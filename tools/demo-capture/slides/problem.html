The problem

Why a sealed auction is hard to verify

01

Checking the price usually opens the bids

Commit–reveal and viewing-key audits make every bid amount readable after the close.

02

Whoever proves it sees every bid

A ZK proof hides bids from the public, but its prover, or an operator who decrypts them, reads the whole book.

03

A proven price doesn’t move the money

The payout is a separate transaction. Unless every payout is proven too, result and money can disagree.

DarkCall · ZECATHON 2026

2

Bidders want their ceilings kept secret, and everyone else wants proof that the price and the payouts are right. Usually one of the two gives way. Commit–reveal opens the bids to check the price. A zero-knowledge proof hides them from the public, but whoever builds the proof still reads every bid. And a proven price does not move the money: unless every payout is proven too, the result and the money can disagree.
