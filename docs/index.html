Verifiable sealed-bid auctions on Zcash

No bid is ever opened.
The price is still proven.
Settled on Zcash.

Not at the close, and not for any single operator. The clearing price is computed on secret shares and proven in zero knowledge, and one shielded transaction pays the seller and refunds every bidder exactly as proven.

Demo scope, up front. The one public number is the clearing price, which by the auction rule equals the highest losing bid or the reserve. Every party runs on one machine, and the local helper that builds a bid's proof sees the typed demo bid. The video and the walkthrough below ran on an isolated Zebra NU7 regtest; one auction also settled on public Zcash testnet. No mainnet funds. The protocol keeps bids from the operators and the public; this demo shows that machinery working, not isolation between separate parties.

How it works

The protocol in six steps

  1. 1

    Bid commitment

    Why can't a bid be changed later?

    The bidder's client commits to a maximum bid and splits it into REP3 secret shares, one pair per operator. A Noir proof shows that the commitment and the shares hold the same bid, within the budget. Each operator checks it and signs a receipt.

    Stays private the bid · Proven commitment and shares agree

  2. 2

    Funding and admission

    Who accepted which bids?

    Each ticket deposits its budget plus fee into a shielded 2-of-3 FROST escrow made by DKG. The operators decrypt the deposits, re-verify the same proofs and shares, and after the close all three sign the root of the accepted book.

    Stays private who funded which ticket · Signed the accepted book

  3. 3

    Confidential clearing

    How is the price computed without seeing the bids?

    The operators run the auction as REP3 multi-party computation on the shares: one uniform price, the winners and every refund. coNoir turns that run into one zero-knowledge proof that the price follows the rules over exactly the committed bids. No bid list is reconstructed.

    Stays private the bid book · Proven price and units sold · Note by the auction rule the price equals the highest losing bid or the reserve

  4. 4

    Private outcome delivery

    Why aren't refunds and winners published?

    Winner flags and refunds leave the MPC as blinded commitments and go only to the settlement step; the public sees the price and the number sold, never the list of results.

    Stays private who won and each refund · Public price and units sold

  5. 5

    Proof-bound Zcash settlement

    How can anyone check the payout matches the result?

    A settlement proof turns the same committed bids into six payment rows: four refunds, the seller and the operator fee. Six Halo2 proofs bind each row's amount and recipient to a native note commitment, and each refund note carries that bidder's encrypted receipt memo. Honest signers sign 2-of-3 only after every proof checks out; colluding signers could still move funds, but not as a verifiable settlement.

    Stays private amounts and recipients · Proven every output matches the result

  6. 6

    Public verification

    What can anyone check without seeing a bid?

    The clearing proof, the six output bindings, the signed accepted book and the native Zcash proofs and signatures, from a public bundle with no secrets. Each bidder verifies a receipt proof, then decrypts only their own memo with their own viewing key.

    Needs no secrets about 100 seconds on a laptop

Proving the price is not enough: DarkCall also proves that the money actually sent matches it. Honest signers sign only after every proof checks out; a payout that differs from the proven result fails public verification, so it cannot pass as this auction's settlement. Designs that open the bids audit the payout afterwards instead.

Trust model and limits.

  • Bid secrecy holds against at most one semi-honest corrupt operator (REP3). Two colluding operators can reconstruct bids. This is not malicious-secure MPC.
  • Custody is 2-of-3 FROST from a DKG. Two colluding signers can move funds, and there is no unilateral refund.
  • Operators hold escrow viewing capability and learn winners and refunds.
  • The public price is the highest losing bid, or the reserve.
  • In the demo, deposits come from one local test wallet right after the close; each bidder's own wallet would deposit in a deployment. Admission signatures attest funding; they are not a ZK funding proof. The bundle does not prove chain inclusion.
  • The circuits have four bid slots and six outputs, and native settlement supports the default terms. Settlement takes about 17 minutes on a 16 GiB M1.
