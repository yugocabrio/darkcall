How it works

How can a sealed auction be publicly verified?

A bid is a willingness to pay. Say a newsletter sells three sponsor slots every month. If a sponsor's ceiling leaks after the close, next month's floor is set from it, and rival sponsors learn each other's budgets. In four stages, DarkCall turns private bids into a provably correct Zcash payment.

Auditable without revealing

Why should an audit reveal the bids?

Blind auctions keep the bids secret but ask everyone to trust the result. Reveal-to-audit designs win the check back by opening the bids. DarkCall keeps the bids secret and still lets anyone check: no bid reveal, no published viewing key, no operator reconstructs the bid book.

Why multi-party ZK proving instead of a single prover?

Take the demo auction: four bidders seal maximum bids of 77, 61, 43 and 21 zatoshi for three units, so the price is 21. A zero-knowledge proof convinces everyone that 21 is right without showing the bids. The question is who has to see the bids to make that proof.

The verifier sees only the public result, not the bids. But the single prover must know every bid to generate the proof.

DarkCall · multi-party ZK proving (coNoir over REP3)
🖥️Operator A holds shares 13🖥️Operator B holds shares 21🖥️Operator C holds shares 32One joint proof

Three independent operators, A, B and C, run the auction. Each bid is split into three secret shares, 123; each operator holds two of them, and one share alone reveals nothing. The three operators compute the witness and the proof together from those shares. No single operator ever holds the complete bid book.

Assumes at most one semi-honest operator (REP3); two colluding operators could reconstruct the bids.

Multi-party ZK proving × Halo2 output binding

Proving the price is half the problem.
Proving the payment is the other half.

A correct auction means nothing if the money goes somewhere else. DarkCall proves both the clearing result and that every payout in a verified settlement matches it. Two colluding signers could still move the escrow, but such a payout fails public verification.

Multi-party ZK · coNoir · BN254Prove the private resultClearing price and six private payout rows, committed with Poseidon2
Halo2 · PallasProve each payout matches4 refunds, seller and fee: each amount and recipient equals one native note commitment
Native Zcash · NU7One shielded transactionAll six payouts settle privately, signed 2-of-3 with FROST

Across two curves. The auction proof lives on BN254, while Zcash's shielded notes are commitments on Pallas, so the auction proof cannot speak about them directly. Each Halo2 proof recomputes one row's BN254 Poseidon2 commitment inside a Pallas circuit and shows that it holds the same amount and recipient as one native note commitment.

What checks what. The Zcash network validates the native transaction. DarkCall's public verifier checks the auction proof, the six output bindings and the accepted book's signatures against operator keys obtained independently of the bundle, with no secrets. All three operators sign the accepted book; any two of the three FROST signers authorize the settlement, and honest signers do so only after every proof checks out.

Why Poseidon2. In an earlier experiment, coNoir's joint proof of the six-row plan took 1,655 s with SHA-256 row commitments and 8.2 s with field-native Poseidon2. Both figures cover that proving step only, not the six Halo2 proofs.

A closer look at the protocol

  1. 1

    Bid commitment

    🧑🏻Each bidder's own client · 🖥️each operator checks

    Why can't a bid be changed later?

    The bidder's client commits to a maximum bid and splits it into REP3 secret shares, one pair per operator. A Noir proof shows that the commitment and the shares hold the same bid, within the budget. Each operator checks it and signs a receipt.

    Stays private the bid · Proven commitment and secret shares agree

  2. 2

    Funding and admission

    🧑🏻Bidders deposit · 🖥️all three operators sign the book

    Who accepted which bids?

    Each ticket deposits its budget plus fee into a shielded 2-of-3 FROST escrow made by DKG. The operators decrypt the deposits, re-verify the same proofs and secret shares, and after the close all three sign the root of the accepted book.

    Stays private who funded which ticket · Signed the accepted book

  3. 3

    Confidential clearing

    🖥️All three operators, together

    How is the price computed without seeing the bids?

    The operators run the auction as REP3 multi-party computation on the secret shares: one uniform price, the winners and every refund. From the same shares they jointly generate the zero-knowledge proof by multi-party ZK proving with coNoir, so even the proving step never gathers the bids in one place. The proof shows the price follows the rules over exactly the committed bids; no bid list is reconstructed.

    Stays private the bid book · Proven price and units sold · Note by the auction rule the price equals the highest losing bid or the reserve

  4. 4

    Private outcome delivery

    🖥️Operators to the settlement step · 🧑🏻each bidder reads only its own

    Why aren't refunds and winners published?

    Winner flags and refunds leave the MPC as blinded commitments and go only to the settlement step; the public sees the price and the number sold, never the list of results.

    Stays private who won and each refund · Public price and units sold

  5. 5

    Proof-bound Zcash settlement

    🖥️Operators prove each payout · any two sign with FROST

    How can anyone check the payout matches the result?

    A settlement proof turns the same committed bids into six payment rows: four refunds, the seller and the operator fee. Six Halo2 proofs bind each row's amount and recipient to a native note commitment, and each refund note carries that bidder's encrypted receipt memo. Honest signers sign 2-of-3 only after every proof checks out; colluding signers could still move funds, but not as a verifiable settlement.

    Stays private amounts and recipients · Proven every output matches the result

  6. 6

    Public verification

    🌐Anyone · 🔑each bidder, for its own receipt

    What can anyone check without seeing a bid?

    The clearing proof, the six output bindings, the signed accepted book and the native Zcash proofs and signatures, from a public bundle with no secrets. Each bidder verifies a receipt proof, then decrypts only their own memo with their own viewing key.

    Needs no secrets about 100 seconds on a laptop

Trust model: what DarkCall does not prove
  • Bid secrecy holds against at most one semi-honest operator (REP3). Two colluding operators can reconstruct the bids. This is not malicious-secure MPC.
  • Custody is 2-of-3 FROST from a DKG. Two colluding signers can move escrowed funds, and bidders have no unilateral refund. Such a payout fails public verification but is not prevented.
  • The three operators hold the escrow's viewing key, which admitting deposits needs: it shows them fixed-size deposits, not bids, and they learn winners and refunds. No viewing key is published, and public verification needs none.
  • The public price is the highest losing bid, or the reserve, so one bid value can follow from it; the operators, who know the winners, can tell whose it is when only one bid lost.
  • Public verification trusts the auction's operator admission keys, which a verifier must obtain independently of the bundle. Funding is certified by those operators' signatures, not proven in zero knowledge, and the bundle alone does not prove chain inclusion.
  • The public check proves the result over the operators' signed book; it cannot show that every funded bid made it in. Each bidder holds all three operators' signed acceptance and checks that its own ticket is in the book, so dropping an accepted bid is visible to that bidder, not to outsiders.
  • In this demo every party runs on one host: each bidder's client (which sees its own typed bid) and all three operators, and one test wallet funds all four tickets.

Common designs compared, not specific projects. Other ZK and MPC auction designs also keep bids private; DarkCall's focus is combining multi-party ZK proving with proof-bound, native shielded settlement on Zcash. Timings from one 16 GiB laptop; testnet coins only.

About this demo
Sealing a bid gives an instant signed pre-check of its proof and secret shares. Fund on Zcash then makes a real deposit for every sealed ticket on an isolated NU7 regtest: budget plus 15,000 zatoshi each, from one local test wallet. The operators admit the deposits under a chain-funded certificate over the same accepted root. Only then does REP3 clear the book, and Settle on Zcash pays out in one shielded transaction. Each bidder's client runs on this computer: it sees its own typed bid and creates the proof and the secret shares; operators receive only secret shares and proofs.
Four tickets are required. After the cutoff, three operators sign the accepted book and run the same secret shares through REP3. The fixed proof and signed root are verified before any ticket's result is opened. Everything runs on one host; the MPC is semi-honest, and privacy assumes at most one corrupt party.
Amounts are demo-sized on purpose. Bids stay within a 100-zatoshi budget, and the fixed 15,000-zatoshi processing fee per ticket pays the settlement's 30,000-zatoshi network fee and a 30,000-zatoshi operator fee output. This is a cryptographic end-to-end demo, not a pricing proposal.
The opening capability stays in page memory, so reloading loses access. Before Settle, your result is checked against the clearing proof on this computer; after Settle, it is decrypted from your ticket's own native shielded memo. This is not production authentication or in-browser proving.

Trust model and limits
  • Bid secrecy assumes at most one semi-honest REP3 party is corrupt. Two colluding parties can reconstruct bids.
  • Custody is 2-of-3. Two signers can move funds, and users cannot force a unilateral refund.
  • The three operators hold the escrow's viewing key (it shows fixed-size deposits, not bids) and learn refunds and who won. Public outcomes can allow inference. No viewing key is published; verifying needs none.
  • Every party of the recorded auction ran on one host; see the chain note below. No malicious-MPC or production-custody claim.
  • Accepted-book signatures attest funding; they are not a public ZK funding proof. The admission authority is configured and trusted separately.
  • The public check proves the result over the operators' signed book; it cannot show that every funded bid made it in. Each bidder holds all three operators' signed acceptance and checks that its own ticket is in the book, so dropping an accepted bid is visible to that bidder, not to outsiders.
  • Item delivery, network anonymity and Sybil resistance are outside this prototype.
No chain inclusion proof in the public bundle.
